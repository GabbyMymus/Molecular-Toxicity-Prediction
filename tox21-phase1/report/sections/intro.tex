\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Assessing the toxicological liabilities of synthetic compounds, environmental pollutants, and pharmaceutical candidates is a cornerstone of modern public health and chemical regulation~\cite{tox21}. Traditional toxicology relies predominantly on low-throughput in vivo animal bioassays, which are ethically fraught, cost-prohibitive, and often fail to scale to the hundreds of thousands of uncharacterized anthropogenic substances currently in commerce. Consequently, international regulatory frameworks and the scientific community have increasingly championed the ``3Rs'' principles---Replacement, Reduction, and Refinement of animal testing---accelerating the demand for robust, interpretable, and scalable \emph{in silico} toxicological profiling.

The Toxicology in the 21st Century (Tox21) initiative, a collaborative inter-agency consortium comprising the National Institutes of Health (NIH), the Environmental Protection Agency (EPA), and the Food and Drug Administration (FDA), established a pivotal high-throughput screening (HTS) benchmark. Tox21 screens a curated library of approximately 10,000 environmental chemicals and approved drugs against a battery of 12 distinct \emph{in vitro} bioassays. These assays interrogate key biological endpoints categorized into two broad functional pathways: seven Nuclear Receptor (NR) pathways (modulating endocrine signaling and xenobiotic metabolism) and five Stress Response (SR) pathways (probing cellular injury, DNA damage, and mitochondrial dysfunction).

Predicting multi-endpoint bioactivity from 2D molecular structures alone poses severe machine learning challenges:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Label Sparsity and Asynchronous Testing:} Compounds in Tox21 were screened across disparate experimental campaigns; thus, not every molecule was assayed against all 12 targets. Unmeasured values in the target matrix represent missing experiments rather than inactive (negative) calls. Naive negative imputation introduces catastrophic label noise, requiring strict target masking.
    \item \textbf{Extreme Class Imbalance:} Active toxic molecules are rare in chemical screening libraries, often comprising only $3\%$ to $15\%$ of tested compounds for a given assay. Standard evaluation metrics such as classification accuracy are deceptively uninformative, mandating the joint analysis of Receiver Operating Characteristic Area Under the Curve (ROC AUC) and Precision--Recall Area Under the Curve (PR AUC)~\cite{saito2015precision}.
    \item \textbf{Chemical Generalization and Chemotype Bias:} Conventional random splitting partitions structurally near-identical analogs across training and test sets, artificially inflating generalization estimates. Evaluating whether models generalize to novel chemical families requires Bemis--Murcko scaffold partitioning~\cite{bemis1996properties,wu2018moleculenet}.
    \item \textbf{Representation Duality:} Molecular structures can be parsed through discrete local topological fragments (e.g., circular fingerprints) or through macroscopic continuous physicochemical properties (e.g., lipophilicity, polar surface area, molecular weight). The degree to which these modalities synergize in tabular models remains a vital empirical question.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Phase 1 Scope and Contributions}
This report details Phase 1 of our molecular toxicity prediction project. The overarching objective of Phase 1 is to construct an authoritative, leak-free, and fully reproducible classical machine learning baseline on the curated Tox21 benchmark ($7{,}709$ unique compounds). Specifically, our primary contributions include:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Disciplined Chemical Representations:} We construct and validate two molecular feature spaces: 2,048-bit Circular Morgan fingerprints (radius 2, ECFP4 equivalent) and a 2,261-dimensional hybrid modality combining Morgan bits with 213 preprocessed 2D RDKit descriptors. All descriptor scaling, imputation, and clipping parameters are strictly learned from training partitions to prevent data leakage.
    \item \textbf{Multi-Model Architectural Benchmark:} We implement, tune, and evaluate three distinct classifier families: L1/L2-regularized Logistic Regression, balanced-subsample Random Forests, and scale-weighted XGBoost.
    \item \textbf{Rigorous Protocol and Reproducibility Safeguards:} We establish a binding engineering contract enforcing row order invariance, cryptographic SHA-256 data manifest verification, and a pre-registered hyperparameter search budget (eight configurations per model) frozen on the scaffold validation split before scoring the test partition.
    \item \textbf{Extensive Empirical Analysis:} Across 432 final test runs (3 models $\times$ 2 feature sets $\times$ 2 splits $\times$ 3 seeds $\times$ 12 endpoints), we quantify the empirical synergy of hybrid representations, isolate per-endpoint bioassay predictability, and measure the profound generalization penalty imposed by scaffold splits.
    \item \textbf{Baseline for Phase 2 GNNs:} By establishing the performance ceiling of classical tabular estimators, Phase 1 establishes the benchmark against which Phase 2 Graph Neural Network (GNN) and Message Passing architectures will be comparatively assessed.
\end{itemize}
