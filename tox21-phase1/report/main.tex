\begin{abstract}
Predicting chemical toxicity in silico is vital for environmental safety, drug discovery, and reducing animal experimentation. The Tox21 benchmark challenges computational models to predict compound activity across 12 toxicological bioassays encompassing nuclear receptor and stress response pathways. Tox21 exhibits pronounced real-world data pathologies: multi-task label sparsity, extreme class imbalance, and substantial out-of-distribution chemotype shifts. In this Phase 1 investigation, we build a disciplined, fully reproducible classical tabular baseline. We evaluate three distinct classifier architectures---Logistic Regression (liblinear), Random Forest, and XGBoost---evaluated on two molecular representations: 2,048-bit Circular Morgan fingerprints (ECFP4 equivalent) and a 2,261-dimensional hybrid modality combining Morgan fingerprints with 213 preprocessed, leak-free 2D RDKit physicochemical descriptors. Preprocessing transformations (imputation, clipping, and standard scaling) are strictly isolated to training partitions. Each model is tuned from a fixed budget of eight configurations, selected exclusively on the scaffold validation split and frozen in git (commit 041892c) before the test split was scored. Across 432 final test evaluations spanning three random seeds, two partitioning regimes (Bemis--Murcko scaffold split vs.\ random split), and 12 endpoints, Random Forest trained on hybrid features has the highest mean scaffold test performance in our runs (mean ROC AUC $0.7762 \pm 0.0020$, PR AUC $0.4035 \pm 0.0042$); XGBoost is within about $0.003$ PR AUC. XGBoost benefits most from the integration of physicochemical descriptors ($\Delta\text{ROC} = +0.0586$, $\Delta\text{PR} = +0.0829$). On hybrid features all three models score lower on the scaffold split than on the random split (ROC AUC lower by $0.085$--$0.109$, PR AUC lower by $0.142$--$0.170$); the two test sets contain different molecules, so this compares two evaluations rather than measuring leakage in a controlled way. This benchmark is the baseline for the Phase 2 graph neural network.
\end{abstract}
